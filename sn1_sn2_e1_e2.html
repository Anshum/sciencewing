Roadmap

Four factors decide

No single factor decides alone. This lesson concentrates on Factor 1: the substrate.

Substrate

Methyl, primary, secondary, or tertiary?

Nucleophile / Base

Strong and small, strong and bulky, or weak?

Solvent

Polar protic or polar aprotic?

Temperature

Low or mild conditions, or prolonged heating?

Step one

Find X. Classify C.

First confirm that a good leaving group such as Cl, Br, I, OTs, or OMs is bonded to an sp³ carbon.

Select the substrate class
Trap: vinylic and aryl halides have X on an sp² carbon, so the usual SN1/SN2/E1/E2 framework does not apply.
Substrate rules

Rule out before you choose

Use the substrate to remove impossible mechanisms before analysing the reagent and conditions.

1°
Expect SN2 — almost always.

Primary / methyl

  • Minimal steric hindrance makes backside attack easy.
  • Primary carbocations are highly unstable, so SN1 and E1 are normally ruled out.
  • Form C–Nu, break C–LG, and invert a stereocentre when relevant.
Exception 1: strong, bulky bases such as KOtBu or LDA can make E2 competitive.
Exception 2: primary allylic or benzylic substrates can ionise because resonance stabilises the carbocation.
2°
The “problem child”.

Secondary

All four pathways are possible. The nucleophile/base and solvent become decisive.

Reagent characterExpect
Good nucleophile, weak/moderate baseSN2
Alkoxide in polar aprotic solventSN2 favoured
Alkoxide in polar protic solventE2 favoured
Very strong base, e.g. acetylideE2
H₂O or ROHSN1 / E1
3°
Rule out SN2 immediately.

Tertiary

  • Three alkyl groups block the backside approach required for SN2.
  • With a strong base, expect E2.
  • With a weak nucleophile/base, ionisation gives SN1 and E1.
  • Heat shifts the SN1/E1 balance toward E1.
Fast decision: 3° + strong base → E2. 3° + weak reagent → SN1/E1.

Teacher flow

Teach it in three moves

01

Classify aloud

Ask students to point to the leaving-group carbon and count how many carbons are directly attached to it.

02

Rule out

Make students state what is impossible before they choose what is likely. This reduces guessing.

03

Flip and explain

Take a vote, flip the card, and connect the answer back to steric hindrance or carbocation stability.
